EXPECTED RESULTS (based on the well-known diamonds dataset)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Linear (price ~ carat + clarity):
  - R^2 around 0.89
  - Residuals vs Fitted shows a strong fan/funnel shape: variance grows with
    fitted price, and a curved (non-linear) pattern is visible. Residuals also
    take large positive values for big stones - the linear slope under-prices
    big diamonds and over-prices small ones.
  - Q-Q plot shows heavy upper tail (kurtosis well above 0).
  - Breusch-Pagan p-value is essentially 0 -> strong heteroskedasticity.
  - Your TA is right: the linear residuals look bad.

Log-log (log(price) ~ log(carat) + clarity):
  - R^2 around 0.96-0.97
  - log_carat coefficient roughly 1.8 -> a 1% increase in carat is associated
    with about a 1.8% increase in price (this is the carat-price elasticity).
  - Residuals vs Fitted is a much more even cloud around 0, with far less
    funnelling.
  - Q-Q plot is closer to the 45-degree line; tails are tamer.
  - Clarity dummies show the expected ordering (IF > VVS1 > ... > I1) with
    each step worth roughly 8-15% in price, holding carat fixed.

CONCLUSION
----------
Both your eyeballs and the formal tests should agree with the TA: the linear
specification has heteroskedastic, curved residuals, and the log-log
specification fixes most of that while also giving you a clean, reportable
elasticity interpretation for the carat coefficient.

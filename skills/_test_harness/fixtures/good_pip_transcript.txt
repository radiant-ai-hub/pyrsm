Interpretation of the PIP scores (NOT a re-interpretation of the regression coefficients — this is a separate question):

1. **carat** is by far the most important predictor with importance 0.920. Randomly shuffling carat reduces the model's R² by about 0.92 — the model essentially collapses without it.
2. **clarity** is a distant second at importance 0.090 — roughly 10x less important than carat by this measure.
3. **color** is third at 0.036, about 2.5x less important than clarity.
4. **table** (0.002) and **depth** (0.001) contribute almost nothing — the model's predictive power barely changes when they are shuffled.

Ratios make this concrete: carat is ~10x more important than clarity, ~25x more important than color, and ~500x more important than depth or table. The model's predictive power is essentially carried by carat alone.

A predictor's PIP score reflects its actual contribution to model fit. PIP gives importance, not direction — for the direction of effect (positive vs negative on price) you need the regression coefficient.

**Note on relative importance.** The predictors in this model are on different scales (carat in carats, clarity as a categorical, depth in percent, etc.). Coefficient magnitudes and PDP shapes are NOT directly comparable across them. PIP measures the drop in R² when each predictor is shuffled, on a scale that is comparable across predictors and across model types — that's why PIP is the right tool for the "which variable matters most" question, and why we lead with it here instead of with the coefficient table.
